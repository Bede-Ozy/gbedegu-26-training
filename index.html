JCI Action Framework 2.0: The 6-Step Cycle

Modernized to guide JCI organizations from ideas to measurable impact. The framework is a continuous learning cycle rather than a rigid linear process.

1 Validate Idea
2 Analyze Problem
3 Design Solution
4 Engage Stakeholders
5 Execute & Monitor
6 Evaluate & Close
Step 1

Validate Idea

Core Question: Is this project worth doing? Explore ideas and verify alignment with JCI priorities, community needs, and local capacity.

Strategic Alignment: Check if the project idea fits JCI's mission and RISE pillars.
Capacity Assessment: Check if the local chapter has the necessary volunteer energy and basic bandwidth.
Key Output: A clear project concept document and formal board decision to proceed.
Step 2

Analyze Problem

Core Question: Do we truly understand the problem? Confirm needs with evidence, analyzing causes, stakeholder groups, and operational risks.

Evidence-based Needs: Use focus groups and local data instead of assumptions.
Root Cause Analysis: Drill down to identify why problems occur using tools like the "5 Whys".
Key Output: A documented evidence-based assessment of the local problem.
Step 3

Design Solution

Core Question: What exactly will we do? Translate analysis into clear objectives, outcomes, indicators, timelines, and resource budgets.

Logical Framework: Differentiate immediate outputs from long-term community outcomes.
Work Breakdown (WBS): Establish specific timelines and itemized resource allocations.
Key Output: A complete, approved project design and operational roadmap.
Step 4

Engage Stakeholders

Core Question: Who must support and authorize the project? Secure internal approvals, corporate sponsorships, and finalize partner roles.

Partnership Agreements: Sign MOU agreements with corporate partners and public entities.
Resource Confirmation: Confirm funding, materials, and volunteer team rosters before starting.
Key Output: Fully authorized project charter and confirmed resources/sponsorships.
Step 5

Execute & Monitor

Core Question: Are we delivering the project effectively? Implement planned activities, monitor milestones, and make adjustments.

Adaptive Management: Track operations weekly to handle problems like fuel or weather issues.
Data Collection: Register beneficiaries and compile operational metrics (hours, resources used).
Key Output: On-schedule project delivery and collected monitoring data.
Step 6

Evaluate & Close

Core Question: What did we achieve and learn? Measure impact results, document lessons learned, audit finance, and close the project.

Impact Assessment: Run satisfaction forms and check outcomes 3-6 months later.
Closure Auditing: Balance budgets, pay vendors, and archive reports for future chapters.
Key Output: Final evaluation report, lessons log, and administrative sign-off.

JCI Action Framework 2.0: Scalable Project Levels

To keep the framework practical, projects are divided into three levels based on scale. Choose the level that matches your project complexity to avoid unnecessary administrative burden.

Level 1

Light Project

"Start simple. Learn by doing."

Characteristics

Local scope & short duration (days to weeks)
Limited or no budget; volunteer-driven
Few or no external partners

Focus & Examples

Focus: Practical delivery, identify community needs, reflect on results.
Examples: Skills workshops, awareness events, pilot campaigns.
Level 2

Standard Project

"Plan better. Deliver stronger impact."

Characteristics

LO or NO scope; medium duration (3-12 months)
Defined budget; multiple partners & stakeholders
Strategic alignment with local priorities

Focus & Examples

Focus: Project proposal, objectives, clear roles, output/outcome tracking.
Examples: Flagship LO projects, structured community bootcamps.
Level 3

Advanced Project

"Fundable. Accountable. Scalable."

Characteristics

LO, NO, or international scope; long duration (6-36 months)
External grant funding (Foundations, corporate CSR)
Formal contracts; higher financial & reputational risk

Focus & Examples

Focus: Full framework application, formal governance, auditing, donor reporting.
Examples: Multi-country NGO initiatives, EU-funded youth structures.

Core Project Management Concepts

Click Triangle Nodes to Explore

Changing any side of the triangle compromises quality.
Scope: The What & How Much Boundary

Scope defines all the work required to complete the project successfully. It is your project's boundary.

Scope Creep: Adding uncontrolled tasks without expanding budget or time. Stop scope creep early to protect your team from burnout.

Time: The Timeline & Schedule Deadline

Projects have a strict start and end. Time management involves planning calendar milestones, tasks duration, and resource scheduling.

Use simple timelines (like Gantt charts) to track milestones and avoid conference day crunches.

Cost: The Budget & Funds Finance

Cost encompasses financial budgets, materials, and volunteer assets.

Always draft itemized budgets. Map costs to sponsorships (cash or in-kind donations) and run audits to build credibility in JCI Nigeria.

Phase 4: Monitoring & Evaluation

Outputs

Quantitative Actions

The immediate, tangible results of your activities. Easy to count but doesn't show long-term change.

Examples: 50 laptops distributed, 100 students registered, 5 classes run.

Outcomes

Qualitative Changes

The actual impact or behavioral shifts in the community. Requires long-term tracking.

Examples: 65% of students secured tech jobs, local coding projects created.
